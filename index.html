Fix On Call

Fix On Call is a roadside assistance platform for Kenya. It connects a stranded driver with the nearest verified mechanic in real time — covering flat tyres, jump starts, fuel delivery, towing and on-site repair — and runs as a marketplace: drivers pay in-app, mechanics are paid out net of a platform commission.

About this document

Its purpose is twofold: to put the business on one page so everyone is working from the same understanding, and to help define it. 01Overview

What the platform is

Fix On Call is a two-sided marketplace. On one side, drivers who have broken down; on the other, a vetted network of mechanics, tow operators and fuel suppliers. The platform's job is to close the gap between them quickly, safely and transparently — with location, identity, price and payment all handled inside one service request. It is organised around four guiding principles.

Open question

The public site states a 12-minute average response and 47-county coverage. Before launch these need to be separated into commitments we can operationally meet and aspirations — a stated SLA we miss is a bigger reputational risk than a modest one we beat.

i.

Speed is the product

A breakdown is an emergency. Everything — location capture, matching, dispatch, arrival — is optimised for time-to-help, and time-to-help is the metric the platform is measured on.

ii.

Verified on both sides

Every mechanic on the network is identity-checked and vetted before taking jobs. Drivers see who is coming. Trust is enforced by onboarding controls, not by reputation alone.

iii.

Transparent pricing

The price is shown and agreed before work starts, and settled in-app. No roadside negotiation while a driver is stranded and has no leverage.

iv.

Built to expand

New service categories, new counties and new countries should be configuration, not rebuilds — payment rails, language and pricing are parameters of the platform, not assumptions baked into it.

02Purpose

Key goals

Fix On Call is not just an app — it is a network, a set of operating standards and a payments flow, delivered through apps. The early version prioritises the core functional requirements needed to enable the goals below; later phases extend from there.

01

Cut time-to-help

Replace phone calls to individual garages, insurer hotlines and word-of-mouth referrals with a single request that reaches the nearest capable provider immediately, with a live ETA.

02

Build a vetted provider network

Recruit, verify and retain enough mechanics, tow operators and fuel suppliers to make coverage real — density of supply, not app features, is what determines response time.

03

Make roadside pricing transparent

Publish a price before work begins, settle in-app, and give both sides a record — removing the pricing opacity that makes drivers distrust roadside help.

04

Keep both sides safe

Protect drivers waiting at the roadside and technicians working in the field, through identity verification, live tracking, check-in protocols and clear escalation.

05

Earn revenue on every completed job

Take a commission on completed services rather than charging providers upfront, so platform income scales with delivered value and providers carry no entry cost.

06

Become the default layer others plug into

Position the platform so insurers, fleets, fuel retailers and hospitals route their roadside events through it — turning a consumer app into infrastructure.

Business model

A commission-based marketplace. Providers onboard free; the platform earns on every completed service transaction.

Driver

Pays the full fee

Settles in-app via M-Pesa at job completion, against a price agreed before work starts. Rates the experience.

→
Fix On Call

Retains a commission

An indicative 15–20% commission on the completed job. No upfront or subscription fees charged to providers.

→
Mechanic

Receives the net payout

An indicative 80–85% of the job value, tracked in an in-app earnings view. Income is tied to jobs completed.

Later revenue lines: insurer partnerships (billing covered roadside events directly), spare parts commission, and subscription tiers for frequent drivers and fleets.

Open question

The 15–20% commission band is indicative and not yet agreed. It needs to be set against what mechanics will actually accept, what the platform costs to run per job, and whether the rate varies by service type (a tow and a jump start have very different economics). Finance to model this alongside the pricing schedule.

03Users

Who it's for

The platform is designed around four user types, each with a workspace scoped to their role. Access boundaries are enforced at the data layer, so each user sees only what their role requires — a mechanic sees the job they accepted, not the customer book.

Drivers

The stranded motorist

A motorist whose vehicle has failed, often at the roadside, sometimes at night. They use the Driver App to request help, see who is coming and how far away they are, and pay when the job is done. Mobile-first, low-patience, frequently on a poor connection.

  • Request help with location captured automatically by GPS
  • See the assigned mechanic's identity, rating and live position
  • Get a price before work starts and pay in-app
  • Keep a service history and rate the outcome

Mechanics

Verified service providers

An independent mechanic, tow operator or fuel supplier working the network on a gig basis. They use the Mechanic App to receive nearby job offers, navigate to the driver, complete the job and track earnings.

  • Onboard with identity, qualification and capability checks
  • Receive and accept or decline nearby job offers
  • Navigate to the driver and update job status
  • Track earnings and payouts in-app
Open question

Do mechanics join as individuals, or can a garage register and manage several technicians under one account? Garage-level accounts change onboarding, payout and liability — and are how supply density is likely to be built quickly.

Operations

Internal platform staff

The internal team running the network day to day, working in the Operations Console. They watch the live map, intervene when a job stalls or no provider accepts, manage onboarding and payouts, and resolve disputes.

  • Live map, active jobs and key operating metrics
  • Manage drivers, providers and the service catalogue
  • Commission settings, payouts and reconciliation
  • Dispute resolution, refunds and incident handling

Partners

Insurers, fleets, fuel & garages

Organisations that route roadside events into the platform or supply capacity to it — insurers with covered breakdowns, fleet operators, fuel retailers, garages and hospitals. Served through the Partner Portal.

  • Raise and track roadside events on behalf of their members
  • Scoped reporting on volume, response times and cost
  • Manage supply commitments and coverage areas
  • Billing arrangements distinct from consumer pay-per-job
Open question

Partnerships and ecosystem readiness (vendor bonding, fuel partnerships, hospitals, insurance) sits with a role that has not yet been briefed to the executive team. The Partner Portal scope stays provisional until that brief happens.

04Architecture

How it fits together

The platform is built around a dispatch core that owns every service request from creation to settlement. The apps and portals sit around it, and third-party services connect through defined integration boundaries.

System of record
Core · Fix On Call dispatch platform

Dispatch Core

REST API · relational database · geospatial index

The authoritative store and engine for the marketplace: driver and provider records, the vetting state of every provider, the service catalogue and pricing, live provider availability and location, every service request and its state transitions, payments and payouts, and the audit trail. Matching and dispatch decisions are made here.

Open question

Build approach is still open: build the core in-house, or start from a configurable marketplace product and extend it. The trade-off is speed to market against long-term control of the matching logic and data. This needs a decision before any development kick-off, and it determines the hosting, staffing and support model that follows.

06Trust & safety

Trust, safety & compliance

The platform sends a stranger to a stranded driver at the roadside, handles their money and their location data, and puts technicians into unfamiliar places at night. Safety and compliance are product requirements, not policies written afterwards.

Provider vetting

Identity documents, qualification and capability checks, and background screening before a provider can take a job — with the vetting state held in the core and re-checked periodically.

Driver safety at the roadside

The driver sees the assigned provider's name, photo and vehicle before arrival, tracks them live, and can share the job with a contact. An emergency escalation path is required in the app.

Field safety for technicians

Protocols for lone working, night jobs and high-risk locations: job check-in and check-out, a distress signal from the Mechanic App, and defined escalation to operations.

Payments integrity

Collection and payout run through regulated mobile money rails. No card data is stored by the platform. Every movement of money reconciles to a job and its commission.

Data protection

Location and personal data handled under the Kenya Data Protection Act: registration with the ODPC, purpose-limited collection, consented use, and location retained only as long as the job requires.

Application security

Encryption in transit and at rest, secure authentication, role-scoped access, an append-only audit trail, and penetration testing before launch and on a recurring schedule.

Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 ODPC registration NTSA — towing & recovery CBK / mobile money rules PCI-compliant payment handling Google Play & App Store policy County business permits
Open question

Two things need to be settled before launch, not after: insurance and liability — who carries the risk when a roadside repair goes wrong, a vehicle is damaged in tow, or a technician is injured on a job — and the penetration testing timeline, which needs a date and a scope tied to the build plan rather than an open commitment.
